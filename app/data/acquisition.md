# Project Northstar Acquisition Plan

Planning memo CD-026 | Version 1.6 | Updated October 12, 2026
Owner: CorpDev | Confidential: M&A group | Next gate: November 12, 2026
All entities, amounts, and transaction assumptions in this memo are fictional.

## 1. Purpose and current authority

Project Northstar covers the proposed acquisition of Harbor Analytics by
Meridian Labs. The transaction remains subject to diligence, board approval,
regulatory analysis, and definitive agreements. No employee should describe
it as completed or contact the target without the deal lead's approval.

The current mandate permits diligence and nonbinding negotiation only.
The working valuation range is $18 million to $24 million on a cash-free,
debt-free basis with normalized working capital. This range is a modeling
assumption, not an approved bid, guaranteed price, or authority to commit funds.
Finance must separately model fees, retention costs, and integration spend.

## 2. Governance and decision gates

CorpDev maintains the master schedule and consolidates workstream findings.
Finance owns valuation and funding analysis; Legal owns transaction documents
and regulatory assessment. Security, People Operations, and Engineering each
appoint a workstream lead with a named alternate.

| Gate | Required evidence | Decision authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diligence launch | NDA, approved scope, advisors, and diligence budget | CorpDev head and Legal |
| Investment review | Workstream findings, valuation sensitivity, and open-risk register | Investment committee |
| Binding commitment | Committee recommendation, final terms, funding plan, and Legal clearance | Board |
| Closing release | Signed agreements and documented satisfaction or authorized waiver of closing conditions | Authorized signatories under board authority |

Only the investment committee can recommend proceeding to the board. Neither
a favorable workstream report nor this memo authorizes an offer, signature,
waiver, employment promise, or other commitment. Material changes after board
approval must return to the board if they exceed its documented mandate.

## 4. Required workstream depth

Finance must reconcile recurring revenue to contracts, invoices, and cash
collections; distinguish contracted revenue from pipeline; and model churn,
renewal timing, and customer concentration. Reconcile debt-like items and
working-capital adjustments rather than relying solely on reported earnings.

Legal maintains the open-items register covering ownership, litigation,
intellectual-property assignment, material contracts, change-of-control
consents, privacy commitments, and potential filing obligations. No workstream
may interpret a missing document as evidence that no obligation exists.

Security assesses identity controls, privileged access, incident history,
backups, and material suppliers. Engineering assesses integration dependencies,
license obligations, operational resilience, and unsupported components.
People Operations models key-person dependency, retention needs, accrued
benefits, and consultation obligations without promising future roles.

## 5. Materiality, unresolved findings, and escalation

Record each finding with evidence, confidence, estimated exposure, mitigation
owner, due date, and proposed transaction treatment. Treat a finding as material
if any of the following applies, even when its financial effect is uncertain:

- A single customer represents more than 20% of annual recurring revenue.
- An unbudgeted liability or required remediation exceeds $250,000.
- Ownership of core intellectual property cannot be substantiated.
- A required material-contract consent is missing or is expected to be refused.
- A potentially reportable data incident remains unresolved.
- A regulatory restriction may prevent the proposed structure or timing.

Escalate material findings to CorpDev and Legal within one business day;
active security concerns also go immediately to the Security lead.
Thresholds trigger review, not automatic rejection or automatic acceptance.
Below-threshold findings must still be aggregated where they share a cause.
The investment committee must explicitly recommend acceptance, mitigation,
repricing, deferral, or withdrawal for each unresolved material risk.

## 6. Deal-room controls and clean-team handling

Keep this plan and workstream outputs within the M&A group. Use the restricted
deal room, named accounts, and approved advisors covered by confidentiality
terms. Being named as a workstream lead does not itself grant system access;
membership must be provisioned through the access-control process.

Legal determines whether competitively sensitive pricing, customer-level data,
or personal information requires a narrower clean-team workspace. M&A group
membership alone does not authorize clean-team access. Share sanitized
aggregates outside that workspace only after Legal clearance. Do not copy
raw diligence data into general collaboration tools or knowledge indexes.

Record downloads and external disclosures in the deal-room register. Wrong
recipients, unexpected public links, or suspected leaks must be reported to
Security and Legal immediately; do not attempt to resolve a leak solely by
asking the recipient to delete the material.

## 7. Signing, integration planning, and communications

Before a board recommendation, Finance must provide base, downside, and delayed
closing scenarios, including integration costs and funding headroom. Legal
must identify required consents and conditions that cannot be waived internally.
Only authorized signatories may execute definitive documents.

Integration planning may identify dependencies and draft Day 1 activities but
must not direct the target's operations, combine customer data, migrate systems,
or implement staffing changes before closing and Legal clearance. A signed
agreement is not evidence that closing conditions have been satisfied.

Do not include the target name, milestones, valuation, or diligence findings
in general company updates. Direct external inquiries to the deal lead and
Legal. Public announcements require Legal and Communications approval and
must reflect whether the deal is proposed, signed, closed, or terminated.

## 8. Pause, termination, and record retention

CorpDev may pause diligence while an unresolved material issue is assessed;
only the authority identified in the current transaction mandate may approve
resumption or termination. On termination, Legal coordinates advisor notices
and contractual return or destruction obligations. IT removes project access
after Legal confirms required preservation and operational handover.

Legal retains the decision record and signed transaction documents for seven
years after closing or termination, or longer under a legal hold. Target data
must follow the NDA's return and destruction provisions; retention of a decision
record is not blanket permission to retain every target document. Record any
legally required retained copies, their custodian, and restricted location.
